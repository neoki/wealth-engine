# EU Data Act access-by-design — live signal (2026-09-14)

## Signal
Article 3(1) of Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 has just crossed its application date. For connected products and related services placed on the EU market after 12 September 2026, product/service data and relevant metadata must by default be easily and securely accessible to the user, free of charge, in a comprehensive, structured, commonly used, machine-readable format and, where relevant and technically feasible, directly accessible.

Primary source: EUR-Lex Regulation (EU) 2023/2854, Articles 3 and 50: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/2854

## Why this is commercially interesting
This is not merely a policy/documentation deadline: it reaches product architecture. A manufacturer launching connected hardware, machinery, sensors, appliances or a related digital service after the cut-off may need to know what data is in scope, what metadata accompanies it, and whether its current API/export/direct-access path satisfies the design requirement.

The timing is unusually good: the obligation became applicable two days before this evidence record.

## Competition / anti-self-deception check
A generic "Data Act readiness checker" is weak. A free public Data Act Checker already offers a sub-3-minute readiness assessment covering connected products, data access, portability, cloud switching and contracts. ComplianceSME also sells a broader Data Act compliance product. There are also open-source reference implementations for user-data-access endpoints.

Therefore Wealth Engine should NOT create another generic checklist or compliance-information website.

## Candidate wedge
Working name: **Data Access-by-Design Gap Test**.

A narrowly technical fixed-price review for manufacturers/providers about to place a connected product or related service on the EU market:
1. inventory generated product/service data + necessary metadata;
2. identify current user-access route (device/API/export/dashboard);
3. test format, accessibility, security and cost assumptions against Article 3(1);
4. flag missing architecture or disclosure elements;
5. return a concise engineering remediation map, not legal advice.

Initial price hypothesis: EUR 299–499 per product line for a manual/AI-assisted review. Higher-value implementation/API work can be quoted only after evidence of demand.

## Validation gate
Do not build a public web experiment yet. First validate that manufacturers pay for a technical gap test despite free compliance checkers and law-firm guidance. Promote only after evidence such as a qualified conversation, explicit request for review, or payment intent.

## Strategic note
This signal overlaps usefully with the CRA reporting signal: connected-product manufacturers may face both Data Act product-data architecture and CRA cybersecurity/reporting obligations. Do not merge them into a broad compliance product yet; preserve narrow wedges until demand shows which pain is monetisable.
