# Validated-Demand Cloning Playbook

## Purpose
Find businesses whose demand and economic mechanism are already proven, then test whether a new entrant can profitably reproduce the mechanism with a smaller wedge, faster execution and minimal operational complexity.

This is not a mandate to clone code, branding or protected assets. Copy the validated customer job, offer structure and acquisition logic.

## Candidate evidence card
Every candidate in this lane must record:

- Comparable business and source.
- Revenue evidence type, date and confidence.
- Acquisition mix: paid, SEO, affiliates, marketplaces, outbound, community, partners, owned audience, other.
- Channel class: PAID_DOMINANT, MIXED_PAID_SEO, SEO_DOMINANT or OTHER.
- Whether the acquisition channel is accessible to a new entrant.
- Approximate price, gross margin, CAC/payback/churn assumptions when estimable.
- Hidden operational burden: support, integrations, compliance, human fulfillment, infrastructure.
- Smallest credible wedge.
- Smallest sellable/manual offer.
- Fastest path to a qualified buyer and payment.
- Evidence that would kill the thesis.

## Score
Score each dimension from 0–5, then apply governance weights:

| Dimension | Weight | What 5/5 means |
|---|---:|---|
| Founder-use fit | 5% | Pablo understands/uses the job and can credibly operate it |
| Revenue proof | 20% | Multiple concrete, recent, credible money signals |
| Demand without exceptional marketing | 10% | Demand survives without celebrity, huge brand or subsidy |
| Acquisition replicability | 20% | New entrant can access the same channel now |
| Solo maintainability | 10% | One operator + automation can run the first version |
| Unit economics | 15% | Plausible positive contribution margin and acceptable payback |
| Entry wedge | 10% | Clear narrow wedge without inventing fake differentiation |
| Time to first sale | 10% | Qualified buyer can be exposed and pay within days |

## Channel interpretation
### PAID_DOMINANT
Positive for validation speed because traffic can often be purchased immediately. Do not promote merely because an incumbent buys ads. Reconstruct the likely CAC/conversion/payback equation for a new entrant.

### MIXED_PAID_SEO
Often a strong demand signal because the category supports both immediate and compounding acquisition. Verify that paid acquisition works without relying on incumbent brand conversion.

### SEO_DOMINANT
Can indicate an excellent durable business, but it is a poor default for the 24-hour revenue objective unless another fast channel exists. Treat as a slower asset-building opportunity, not an automatic rejection.

### OTHER
Name the actual distribution mechanism and decide whether it is structurally accessible. Founder audience, exclusive partnerships, app-store ranking or proprietary data can make superficially simple businesses hard to reproduce.

## Research order
1. Find credible proof that money is changing hands.
2. Determine exactly where customers come from.
3. Decide whether that distribution is accessible to a new entrant.
4. Estimate unit economics before feature analysis.
5. Inspect operational complexity and support burden.
6. Find the narrowest credible wedge.
7. Design the smallest paid/manual experiment.
8. Only then consider building software.

## Default experiment
Prefer:
`proven demand → tiny offer → real distribution → payment → manual fulfillment → automate repeated work`

Avoid:
`idea → product → infrastructure → launch → search for customers`

## Promotion logic
A high score creates research priority, not permission to build a full product. The candidate must still pass Wealth Engine's commercial activation gate and 24-hour survival rule. One payment validates willingness to pay; repeated acquisition at acceptable economics validates a business.

## Anti-patterns
- “Nobody is doing this” presented as evidence.
- Revenue screenshots with no attempt to understand costs.
- Paid ads treated as proof of profitable ads.
- SEO success assumed reproducible in a week.
- Cloning a broad incumbent instead of isolating a wedge.
- Simple UI mistaken for simple operations.
- Building the SaaS before testing the offer.
