# Sweep 18 — mundane spend / distribution asymmetry

## Directive
Start outside compliance, recovery, routes and DPP. Prefer mundane recurring jobs with observable spend or distribution asymmetry. Do not force queue entry.

## WORLD samples
Sampled laundry/linen, dental/veterinary reception, rental turnover/inspection, restaurant operations/menu economics, vending/office service, pools/property care, construction equipment, plants, coffee/warewashing maintenance, beauty inventory, key/access management and related recurring operations.

## Evidence and decisions

### Laundry / linen operations — proven, but operationally heavy and occupied
Spain has pickup/delivery consumer laundry and B2B linen contracts. WashMe publishes B2B bands around €3.75–€5.75/kg wholesale and €6.49/kg volume; DropMe operates pickup laundry in Barcelona/Alicante. Elis operates 39 industrial laundries in Spain across hospitality, healthcare, industry and services. This validates recurring spend but also shows capital/logistics density and strong incumbency. No queue entry.

### Dental/veterinary recall and reception — rapidly commoditizing
Multiple Spain-facing products already automate WhatsApp booking, reminders, no-show reduction, patient reactivation and AI reception; veterinary products bundle booking/reminders and even consultation-to-recall workflows. The obvious 'missed appointment / recall' outcome layer is already becoming software commodity. No queue entry.

### Rental inspection / turnover / access — integrated
Spain-serving operators already combine turnover cleaning, linen, calendar sync, photo proof, inspections, smart locks, guest registration and compliance. Check Easy reports >2,000 properties equipped and Handover sells photo-documented handovers in Spain. Generic orchestration wedge is occupied. No queue entry.

### Restaurant profitability / menu engineering — economically meaningful, but software is collapsing price
Consultants sell menu engineering and restaurant profitability work, demonstrating real willingness to pay and measurable margin impact. However current software already imports sales/invoices, computes food cost, menu engineering and suggested actions at roughly €6–€99/month; RistoBrain even offers done-for-you setup/menu engineering for €250 one-off. Generic 'POS export -> AI profitability recommendations' is therefore not queue-worthy.

### Equipment uptime as subscription — reusable primitive, not yet opportunity
Coffee-machine and warewashing providers demonstrate a useful model: fixed recurring fee + preventive maintenance + priority repair + parts/service discounts; some remotely monitor equipment and sell uptime rather than software. Spain pool maintenance similarly demonstrates recurring €60–€145/month physical care. The primitive is `small-business critical asset -> predictable downtime pain -> preventive subscription -> priority repair -> fixed/predictable cost`. Keep as WORLD primitive; search later for fragmented asset categories where OEM/dealer service has not already integrated the subscription.

### Office plants / vending / pools — proven but mature service categories
All-inclusive install/maintain/replace models and turnkey vending are already standard. These are useful proven-model sources but no obvious asymmetric entry was found.

## Sweep conclusion
No candidate earned active-queue entry. The most useful new search primitive is not another reporting/AI layer but **uptime-as-a-service for mundane revenue-critical SME equipment**, where the customer pays for continuity and response priority rather than analytics. The next sweep should test this primitive across unrelated equipment categories while preserving at least half of WORLD sampling outside equipment/services to avoid a new attractor.

## Meta-learning
Sweep 18 repeatedly found that cheap vertical SaaS now absorbs obvious data-to-recommendation opportunities extremely quickly. A stronger filter is required before researching a software idea deeply: ask whether a €20–€100/month vertical SaaS already performs 70%+ of the proposed loop. If yes, rotate unless we possess distribution, proprietary data, liability/outcome ownership or a materially different economic model.
