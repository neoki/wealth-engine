# Sweep 15 — unrelated WORLD needs

## Purpose
Break the recovery/refund/waste/route/cost-optimization attractor. Sample needs first across unrelated domains, then map business models. No build action.

## Worlds sampled
Household evidence/inventory; bereavement administration; school uniforms/materials; restaurant allergen/menu operations; equipment rental; holiday-home/keyholding; tourist laundry; menu photography; digital legacy; senior transitions; estate liquidation/consignment; specialist resale; family handoffs.

## Findings

### 1. Bereavement administration / life-event orchestration — OBSERVED, not promoted
The job is objectively fragmented: after death families may need certificates, last wills, banking/testamentary processes, inheritance tax, pensions, vehicle transfers, utilities/subscriptions and other private-provider changes. Spanish official/bank guidance confirms multiple independent procedures and deadlines.

A UK analogue, Settld, demonstrates the product primitive `tell once -> notify many organizations -> track status`, but Spain is not empty. Funeral providers and gestorías already offer post-mortem administration; insurers may include legal/post-mortem/digital-life services; HeredaYa sells a digital inheritance checklist/plan; Mi Legado Digital covers digital/post-mortem workflows. UNESPA reports 22.3m people (45.6% of Spain) had death insurance in 2024, with complementary services sometimes included.

Conclusion: pain is strong and event-driven, but a generic D2C `death admin concierge` is not yet a good entry. Preserve a narrower possible mutation for later: white-label workflow/status layer for small funeral homes or advisors only if evidence shows they currently coordinate these tasks manually and would pay rather than already receiving the capability from insurers/software.

### 2. School-uniform/material reuse — OBSERVED-OCCUPIED
Spain already has SCOOL and Wetudy connecting families by school; schools/AMPAs also run physical uniform markets. Internationally, Retykle demonstrates a more managed school-partner resale model where the school batches supply. The interesting primitive is `institution creates a closed, recurring two-sided market and batches acquisition`, but current ticket sizes and strong free/community norms make monetization unclear. Do not build another marketplace.

### 3. Household inventory / claim readiness — OBSERVED-CROWDED
Multiple current products use AI/photo capture to inventory household goods, receipts, warranties and insurance evidence. Product need is real but software supply is abundant and often free/freemium. No entry without a privileged insurer/property-manager channel or a materially different job.

### 4. Restaurant allergen/menu digitization — OBSERVED-CROWDED
Several current products already ingest menus/PDFs, map EU allergens, translate and publish QR menus. No generic entry.

### 5. Sell-it-for-me vertical — maturation round
Full-service consignment is proven in Spain across books, clothing, cars and general high-value objects. Micobooks collects books and handles valuation/listing/shipping; Olokuti consigns selected clothing; Zona Carpe Diem offers collection + eBay selling for items worth >= EUR300 in a limited Spanish geography; The Circular Market handles whole-home clear-outs through managed at-home sales.

This validates the job but weakens the generic candidate: the model naturally fragments into verticals because authentication, storage, logistics, merchandising and buyer trust differ by category. Classification remains POSSIBLE-ROTATED. Revisit only when a specific high-value vertical has poor liquidity/service coverage and enough gross value per intake.

## New primitives retained
- `life event -> many organizations -> one evidence bundle -> status orchestration` can be valuable even when each underlying procedure already exists.
- `institution-defined closed market -> repeated predictable supply/demand -> batched handoff` can create distribution, but free/community norms can destroy monetization.
- Full-service consignment is not one market; category-specific trust/logistics determine economics.

## Anti-attractor result
The ban worked: this sweep began from unrelated human/operational jobs rather than money recovery or physical routes. No candidate deserves promotion merely because the search space was diversified.

## Decision
No BRILLIANT/PROMISING promotion. Rotate sell-it-for-me. Keep bereavement workflow only as an observed problem primitive pending explicit B2B workflow evidence. Next sweep should remain broad and sample business creation/operations, family logistics, education, hospitality, property transactions, professional services and niche commerce without seeding from the last candidate.
