# Sweep 08 — grave-care maturation + fresh WORLD — 2026-09-19

## Candidate: grave-care-subscription-galicia

### Round 2 — local supply gap
Falsification weakened the whitespace thesis. Pontevedra already had firms offering periodic grave cleaning, flowers and photo confirmation in 2013; a current funeral-services operator (Gesporten) also advertises periodic cemetery cleaning/maintenance. This is not a novel category in Galicia. However, current web discovery does not show a dominant Galicia-wide digital brand focused on remote descendants.

Evidence: PontevedraViva (2013) described monthly/quarterly/semiannual services, flowers, photo proof and ~€100/year basic maintenance. Current Gesporten advertises periodic or one-off grave/niche/pantheon care. Campa.care validates a modern digital version at €35/month including monthly cleaning, natural flowers, photo report and app tracking.

### Round 3 — demand geometry
The diaspora thesis is unusually strong in raw population terms: IGE reports 563,303 Galicians registered abroad on 1 Jan 2026; 78.4% live in the Americas and 20.8% in Europe. But 76.6% were born abroad and only 22.6% in Galicia, so the addressable population cannot be equated with 563k customers. Emotional attachment and ownership/knowledge of a family grave are unverified.

### Round 4 — route/unit economics
The service only becomes attractive with cemetery-level batching. Historic Galicia pricing (~€100/year for four grave cleans) implies only ~€25/visit before flowers/travel; modern Campa pricing (€35/month with flowers) is much stronger but would still be fragile if visits require long rural drives. Galicia's fragmented parish-cemetery geography can destroy route economics. The right operating unit is therefore a cemetery cluster, not a province.

At €35/month, 100 subscriptions = €3,500 MRR. If 100 monthly visits could be concentrated into 4 dense cemetery days, revenue density is attractive; if spread over dozens of rural cemeteries, it is poor. This remains an unproven assumption, not a forecast.

### Round 5 — acquisition
The best plausible channels are diaspora associations/Centros Galegos, funeral homes, florists, stonemasons and cemetery-adjacent providers. Xunta evidence confirms a very large organized exterior community, but no evidence yet proves a cheap repeatable commercial acquisition channel. Paid consumer acquisition could easily overwhelm a €35/month service.

### Classification
**POSSIBLE+ → POSSIBLE-ROTATED.** Real demand, proven pricing and a large remote-family population exist, but local competition is not absent and route density/acquisition remain decisive unknowns. Do not spend more desk-research cycles. Revisit only if a dense cemetery/channel wedge appears externally.

## Fresh WORLD observations

### 1. Wheelie-bin cleaning subscription — UK proven, Spain fit weak
UK operators sell 4-weekly bin cleaning at roughly £4–£10/month and one network claims 230+ towns. The model has excellent route density because cleaning is synchronized to municipal collection. Spain/Galicia commonly uses different waste-container patterns, so copying without local household-bin density evidence is unjustified. **Observation only.**

### 2. Baby-equipment rental to tourists — proven Spanish local model
Multiple Spanish tourism markets rent cots, strollers, high chairs and car seats for roughly €15–€55/week/item, often with delivery/collection. This is a boring proven model with hotel/holiday-rental distribution potential but inventory, cleaning, safety/liability and logistics. **POSSIBLE observation**, especially where tourism density and accommodation referral channels are strong.

### 3. Reusable moving-box rental — already operating in Spain
Spanish operators rent reusable plastic moving crates from roughly €0.99–€1.50/box/week and add delivery/collection. Simple proven circular-rental model; economics depend on asset turns, losses and dense delivery radius. **POSSIBLE observation**, not priority yet.

### 4. Knife-sharpening/renting subscription — strong model, Galicia already occupied
Valencia operator CORTÉS sells restaurant subscriptions at €79/€169/€449 per month. Fresh Galicia search found KatShin in Pontevedra already offering periodic restaurant pickup, monthly contracts and knife rental at €10 + VAT per knife/month. This is exactly the kind of profitable copy Wealth Engine should notice, but Galicia is not whitespace. **Do not pursue locally without a distribution/segment advantage.**

## Durable learning
A geographic-copy hypothesis must test local operating geometry, not merely local competitor count. Route businesses are governed by revenue per stop/km/hour; a large TAM can be worthless when service points are geographically fragmented. Conversely, synchronization anchors (bin day, hotel turnover, restaurant route, cemetery cluster) can create the density that makes a mundane service excellent.

## Next
Rotate away from grave care. Continue WORLD sampling. Mature baby-equipment rental only if a channel-first angle (hotels/holiday rentals/property managers) can plausibly remove direct-to-consumer CAC and logistics can be concentrated geographically.
