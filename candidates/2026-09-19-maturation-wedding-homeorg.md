# Maturation — Wedding distribution + Home Organization — 2026-09-19

## Wedding Content Creator Network — acquisition/channel round

### New evidence
- Bodalia publicly offers wedding suppliers a free listing and paid plans at €499/year and €899/year, with no per-lead commission. This proves suppliers pay for wedding demand, but also means generic directory acquisition is already competitive.
- FiestaGo positions itself against Bodas.net/Zankyou as transaction-first/no supplier subscription, and publicly cites incumbent annual supplier spend of roughly €600–€2,500 (Bodas.net) and €500–€1,800 (Zankyou). Treat these numbers as competitor-provided, not audited facts.
- Weddingvenues.es explicitly targets UK/Ireland destination-wedding couples in Spain and sells supplier sourcing; it argues international couples have a harder trust/search problem.
- Spain Wedding Tourism Association reports >3,200 international weddings in Spain in 2024 and +62% growth over three years, with average destination-wedding spend €80k–€250k. This suggests a higher-value niche than generic local weddings.
- Current customer discussion remains ambivalent: some couples value 24h delivery strongly, while others see content creators as redundant beside photo/video. A recent videography discussion identifies delivery speed as the actual wedge and also highlights substitution risk if videographers add fast vertical deliverables.
- Spanish pricing remains extremely dispersed: P2P Eventos €250–€320; Ventura €1,200 + VAT; Ram a l'Aire €1,028.50–€1,573. This is category formation, not stable pricing.

### Interpretation
The generic Galicia network is not yet BRILLIANT. Acquisition via broad wedding portals can become paid and competitive, and the service itself is easy for photographers/videographers to bundle. The stronger mutation is **Spain destination-wedding rapid-content network**, initially English-speaking/international couples, where trust, replacement coverage, bilingual coordination and 24h SLA matter more and ticket size is less constrained.

### Classification
PROMISING+, but rotate after this round unless genuinely new evidence arrives. Do not build yet. The next decisive evidence should be external buyer/channel evidence, not more desk research. Candidate can become BRILLIANT only if a low-CAC distribution wedge is demonstrated (planner/venue/photographer partnerships, destination-wedding channel, or organic inbound) with contribution margin >=35% after creator payout/travel/QA.

## Professional Home Organization — first maturation rounds

### New evidence
- Home Atelier Mallorca publishes €600–€960 per space, €1,500–€4,000+ per project, €1,100–€3,000 arrival resets and €350–€650 recurring care visits.
- SimpleLife Organizers publishes €45/hour home organizing and €480–€1,200/month property-management subscriptions.
- Fixando shows home-organization requests/price pages across Madrid, Málaga, País Vasco and Galicia, generally €300–€600 fixed-price, with a Galicia example averaging €450. This supports national demand but also shows that ordinary home organization is often a modest-ticket labor service rather than a luxury-margin category.
- Standard domestic cleaning is much cheaper (~€20/hour recurring in one current Barcelona offer), so premium organization must sell transformation/decluttering/move-in/second-home outcomes rather than hours.

### Interpretation
The Mallorca luxury pricing does not generalize cleanly to Galicia/Spain. The scalable opportunity, if any, is not generic closet organization. Better mutations are high-ticket event-driven packages: move-in reset, downsizing/estate clear-out, pre-sale declutter/staging, second-home arrival setup, or B2B referral relationships with movers/realtors/interior designers.

### Classification
POSSIBLE. Needs a second independent round on event-driven demand + referral distribution + labor economics before promotion. Do not build.
