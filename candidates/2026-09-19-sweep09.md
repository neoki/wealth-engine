# Sweep 09 — 2026-09-19

## Baby equipment rental
Round 2, channel: Spanish operators already deliver to hotels, villas, holiday rentals and airports. A broad marketplace also operates across many Spanish cities. The hoped-for property-manager/hotel delivery wedge is therefore normal category behavior, not differentiation.

Round 3, economics: observed stroller/cot/car-seat prices are commonly about EUR25–55/week, while delivery can add EUR5–25 or be bundled. Inventory can amortize over repeated hires, but single-item orders leave limited room for delivery, collection, cleaning, storage, damage and partner commission. Density and basket size dominate.

Round 4, operations: child seats add compliance, fitting and safety-check expectations; cots/strollers still require hygiene and damage controls.

Classification: POSSIBLE-ROTATED. Proven demand, but the initial channel insight is occupied. Revisit only with privileged distribution or an unusually dense underserved tourist micro-market. Do not build.

## Fresh WORLD
Solar-panel maintenance: Spanish offers show residential cleaning from roughly EUR89–200/visit and annual plans around EUR150–400. Community services already sell through property administrators. Retain one mutation for testing: a channel operator that aggregates administrator demand and routes work to qualified specialists while owning scheduling/reporting/renewal. POSSIBLE, not yet matured.

Rug cleaning: a Spanish operator offers a standardized EUR69.99 nationwide pickup-clean-return service and reports 15,000+ customers. The category is occupied, but the reusable primitive is valuable: standardize + collect + outsource transformation + return.

Sneaker cleaning: current Spanish prices roughly EUR15.50–55/pair with pickup/delivery. Real demand, weak ticket unless batches manufacture density. Low priority.

Laundry marketplace: the obvious marketplace mutation is already being pursued in Spain; pickup/wash/return is also established. Occupied.

## Learning
For route/logistics businesses, require at least one density engine: many jobs at one node, a partner that batches demand, or enough ticket/gross margin to absorb two logistics legs. Delivery itself is not a moat or channel wedge.

Next: rotate baby rental. Test solar-maintenance channel economics once while sampling unrelated WORLD domains; avoid turning route services into the next attractor.
