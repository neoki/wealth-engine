# Maturation — Wedding Content Network vs Home Watch — 2026-09-19

## Wedding Content Creator network / Galicia

### Round A — demand and price
- Galicia recorded 9,348 marriages in 2024; Pontevedra province alone recorded 3,382. 2025 Q3 had 3,543 marriages in Galicia, +1.72% YoY.
- Current Spanish WCC offers span a very wide price band: P2P €250–320; Team Bride/Lala from €490; Say I Do Social €500/€900; Ventura €1,200 + VAT; Ram a l'aire €1,028.50–1,573. This validates real willingness to pay but also indicates an immature category without settled pricing.
- Traditional Galician wedding video starts around €1,250 at Visualtec and delivery is typically 3–6 months, strengthening WCC's immediate-delivery wedge rather than requiring it to replace professional video.

### Round B — distribution
- Spain already has wedding supplier directories/aggregators (Bodas.net, Team Bride) and planners/venues that can act as channels. Team Bride has a national WCC category; Bodas.net has individual WCC listings. This lowers category-education cost but also means distribution is not empty.
- A network has a real structural advantage over solo creators: geographic coverage, backup creator when someone is ill, standardized deliverables, availability across simultaneous dates, and channel partnerships. This is the candidate wedge—not inventing WCC.

### Round C — economics / scalability
- The market price band permits a hypothetical agency structure, but creator payout/CAC are not publicly established enough to claim attractive margins yet.
- Seasonality and Saturday concentration create both a constraint and a network advantage: a solo creator caps out at one wedding/date; a network can parallelize.
- Key unknown: whether trained creator supply can be recruited at a payout that leaves >=30–40% gross contribution after editing/QA/acquisition.

### Round D — creator supply economics (fresh evidence)
- Spain 2026 freelance audiovisual benchmark: video content creator median ~€178/day, P25 ~€141 and P75 ~€226; video editor median ~€167/day. This is not wedding-specific pay, but it provides an external labor-cost anchor.
- Against observed WCC retail prices of €490 to €1,573, creator-day cost is not intrinsically fatal. At €690 retail and €225 creator payout, €465 remains before editing, CAC, travel, QA and overhead; at the low €250–320 retail tier, agency economics are much less attractive.
- Therefore the network should not compete at the cheapest end. A standardized mid/premium package plus creator payout per event is economically plausible on desk evidence.
- Team Bride already aggregates selected WCC providers nationally, proving that discovery/aggregation is not novel. The stronger network hypothesis must own booking, standardized SLA/output and backup fulfillment rather than merely list creators.

### Classification
PROMISING+, not BRILLIANT. Supply economics are now plausible rather than purely assumed, but repeatable low-CAC acquisition and actual creator willingness at event payouts remain unproven.

## Second-home Home Watch / Costa Blanca

### Round A — demand and geography
- Alicante/Costa Blanca is a much stronger launch geometry than Galicia because foreign/non-resident ownership is dense.

### Round B — price and competition
- Current operators prove recurring spend and strong price dispersion/competition.

### Round C — economics / moat
- Route density is decisive. Trust, local presence, multilingual service and referral channels matter more than software. Best hypothesis: B2B2C post-sale property care for foreign owners.

### Round D — micro-geography / route economics (fresh evidence)
- Costa Blanca South pricing is very dense and low: AKEE keyholding €89/year plus visits; Sunny Key monthly inspection €340/year (~€28/month); Daniela €35/month for one monthly check; Costakey €45/month; KwiCo €30/month; Costa Blanca Villa Services €69/month. This is stronger evidence of demand but worse evidence for attractive standalone inspection margins.
- Many providers already bundle keyholding, inspections, technician access, cleaning/rental coordination and multilingual support. Real-estate/aftercare positioning is also already present in the market. Therefore B2B2C is not an empty wedge.
- A simple route model illustrates the constraint: 100 homes at €45/month = €4,500 monthly revenue. If each receives one visit/month, that is 100 physical stops plus reporting and exceptions before overhead. Upsells and very tight geographic clustering are necessary.
- Higher tiers (€100–400/month in Axarquía; €149/month premium Costa Blanca examples) prove premium willingness to pay, but those tiers include higher service intensity and emergency/incident management.

### Classification
PROMISING-, downgraded relative to Wedding Network. Demand is proven but the core service is operationally heavy and aggressively priced; a profitable entrant needs exceptional route density, channel economics or high-margin add-ons. Do not build yet.

## Fresh WORLD observations (anti-attractor)
- Mobile detailing: current Costa del Sol operators sell €150 full detailing and one operator advertises a €600/month VIP package for four premium washes. Proven high-ticket mobile convenience; POSSIBLE as a replicable local-service model, not yet matured.
- Professional home organization: a Mallorca operator publicly prices individual spaces €600–960, projects €1,500–4,000+, arrival resets €1,100–3,000 and ongoing care €350–650/visit. This is a surprisingly high-ticket, low-tech service category; POSSIBLE and worth geographic/copyability maturation.
- Solar-panel cleaning: Spanish operators show ~€100–250 one-off residential cleans and annual maintenance plans from €250/year; route density and roof-safety constraints dominate. POSSIBLE but lower priority.
- Pool maintenance: Costa Blanca has abundant recurring contracts around €60–135/month, proving demand but also intense local competition and labor intensity. Useful proven-business input, not currently a top candidate.

## Portfolio decision
1. Wedding Content Network remains the strongest current candidate and receives one acquisition/channel round before any BRILLIANT decision.
2. Home Watch remains in portfolio but should rotate after this round; no more desk research until a genuinely new channel/economic insight appears.
3. Fresh WORLD candidates enter maturation rather than allowing weddings/property to monopolize the engine.
