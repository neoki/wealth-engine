## AP recovery audit — round 2: economics/channel

Public recovery-audit benchmarks cluster around roughly 0.05%–0.5% of audited supplier spend for a first audit, with contingency fees commonly around 20%–30% of recovered cash. These are industry/vendor benchmarks, not guaranteed Spanish-SME outcomes.

Implication: at €1m audited supplier spend, gross recovery might be only ~€500–€5,000, yielding roughly €100–€1,500 of total contingency revenue before advisor revenue-share and manual claim work. At €5m spend, the same benchmark geometry becomes ~€2,500–€25,000 recovered and ~€500–€7,500 gross fee. This makes the original broad SME framing weak: the lower tail cannot economically support manual validation, supplier contact and advisor sharing.

The advisor channel is plausible but not automatically privileged. Spain already has recovery/collection providers distributing through gestorías; one debt-recovery provider publicly claims 150+ gestorías and explicitly shares recovered-value economics. That validates advisors as a channel while also proving channel competition exists. AP recovery remains distinct from ordinary debt collection, but 'advisor distribution' itself is not a moat.

Decision: downgrade from POSSIBLE+ to POSSIBLE-ROTATED. Revisit only with evidence of a high-spend SME/mid-market segment, an unusually cheap automated claim workflow, or privileged advisor access to portfolios where supplier spend can be prequalified. Do not build.

## Unrelated WORLD observations

### 1. Expiring employee-training entitlement (FUNDAE) — real value, occupied
FUNDAE confirms Spanish employers receive annual training credit based on prior professional-training contributions and workforce size; firms under 50 can reserve unused credit for the following two years if they elect to do so by the deadline. A mature ecosystem of organizing entities already calculates credit, manages paperwork, provides training and markets the avoidance of losing unused credit. Strong primitive, weak generic entry.

Classification: OBSERVED-OCCUPIED.

Reusable primitive: `expiring entitlement + deadline + data-holder advisor + service that converts entitlement into useful outcome`. Search other entitlement classes later, but do not turn the next sweep into subsidies/recovery.

### 2. E-commerce chargeback recovery — proven, crowded
Multiple 2026 services already automate dispute detection, evidence assembly/submission and result tracking with 7.5%–25% success fees or hybrid subscription/success pricing. The complete recovery loop is already productized for small merchants.

Classification: OBSERVED-OCCUPIED.

### 3. Restaurant no-show/deposit recovery — occupied
Spain-facing reservation products already combine online/WhatsApp reservations, deposits, reminders and no-show reduction. No generic wedge.

Classification: OBSERVED-OCCUPIED.

### 4. EPR compliance for cross-border e-commerce — emerging but already productizing
New platforms connect Shopify/WooCommerce, map country/product-stream obligations and coordinate registration/reporting for European EPR. Interesting regulatory tailwind, but generic compliance aggregation is already forming and would pull the engine back toward familiar compliance SaaS.

Classification: OBSERVED-EMERGING; no candidate.

### 5. Returns liquidation as a service — proven foreign model
Operators receive non-restock customer returns, scan/reconcile/prep/liquidate them and remit recurring proceeds. This proves a useful physical-commerce primitive: `exception inventory -> outsourced disposition -> cash recovery`. No Spain/channel evidence strong enough in this pass to create a candidate.

Classification: WORLD-PRIMITIVE.

### 6. Business insurance claim advocacy — economically plausible, professionally occupied
Spanish commercial claims have meaningful average payouts and independent experts charge material fees, but brokers, loss adjusters and peritos already sit naturally in the workflow. No evidence of an unowned scalable layer found in this pass.

Classification: OBSERVED-OCCUPIED/PROFESSIONAL.
